Content Refresh Prioritization

Machine Learning Decision-Support for Content Review

A time-aware machine learning study that identifies measurable future-decline signals and converts them into an interpretable ranked review queue using historical search visibility and engagement measurements.

Research Question: Which content pages show the strongest measurable signals of refresh opportunity, and how can those pages be ranked for human review using historical search and engagement data?

Research Summary

Abstract

This research investigates whether historical search and engagement signals can help identify content pages that deserve earlier human review because they later experience substantial declines in search visibility.

Using March 2026 FlyRank internship warehouse data, the workflow separates historical features from future outcomes, applies client-grouped validation, audits potential leakage, trains an interpretable Decision Tree classifier, and converts predicted decline-risk probabilities into a ranked content-review queue.

The final model achieved 72.71% accuracy , 57.98% precision , 12.71% recall and an F1 score of 20.85% on held-out clients. The results are interpreted as directional decision-support rather than proof that an individual page requires refreshing.

01 — Research Problem

Prioritizing Content Review at Scale

Large content portfolios can contain thousands of pages, making it difficult for content teams to manually review everything with equal attention. This research evaluates whether historical performance signals can help identify a smaller set of pages that deserve earlier investigation.

Decision to Support

The system answers one practical question: which pages should a content team investigate first? It is not designed to automatically decide that a page should be deleted, rewritten, merged, or refreshed.

03 — Methodology

Time-Aware ML Workflow

Historical features and future outcomes are deliberately separated to reduce leakage risk. Features are created only from information available before the prediction point, while later data is reserved for defining the observed future outcome.

1

Historical Window

March 1–21, 2026 is used to construct historical search and engagement features.

2

Future Outcome

March 22–31 is used only to measure subsequent changes in daily impressions.

3

Grouped Validation

Clients are separated between training and testing so that no client appears in both sets.

4

Action Engine

Predicted decline-risk probabilities are converted into human-readable review priorities.



Historical Model Features

DAILY_IMPRESSIONS CTR AVG_POSITION DAILY_SESSIONS ENGAGEMENT_RATE DAILY_SCROLL_EVENTS

Target Definition

A page is marked as an observed future-decline case when its average daily future impressions are at least 20% lower than its historical daily impressions. Future-window variables are not provided to the model as predictive features.

Validation Design

GroupShuffleSplit was used with clients as the grouping variable. The final evaluation contained 36 training clients , 9 held-out clients , and zero client overlap between the two sets.

04 — Evaluation

Model Results

A shallow Decision Tree is evaluated against a majority-class baseline on completely held-out clients. The analysis focuses on accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score, confusion-matrix behavior, and model feature reliance rather than using accuracy alone.

Baseline vs Decision Tree

The majority baseline detects no future-decline cases. The Decision Tree begins identifying a measurable subset while maintaining similar overall accuracy.

Feature Importance

Feature importance represents how much the trained model relied on each historical signal. It should not be read as evidence of causal influence.

Main Finding

Useful precision, limited recall

Precision of 57.98% indicates that the smaller group of pages flagged by the model contains useful measured signal. However, recall is only 12.71%, meaning that many pages that later experience substantial decline are still missed. The model is therefore more appropriate for prioritizing a focused review queue than for comprehensive decline detection.

Ranked Review Queue

Human-Readable Reason Codes

Individual pages can receive one or more reason codes describing the historical measurements associated with their review priority.

HIGH_VISIBILITY_AT_RISK LOW_CLICK_CAPTURE WEAK_SEARCH_POSITION LOW_ENGAGEMENT DECLINE_RISK_SIGNAL

Reason codes make the queue easier to inspect, but they do not prove why a page declined or guarantee that a specific content edit will improve performance.

Why this is a review queue

A high risk score means review the page first , not automatically refresh the page . Search demand, seasonality, technical issues, indexing, analytics coverage, page purpose, and content quality should all be inspected before any material change is made.
