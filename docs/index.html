Content Refresh Prioritization

Machine Learning Decision-Support for Content Review

A time-aware machine learning workflow for identifying content pages showing measurable future-decline signals and ranking them for human review using historical search visibility and engagement data.

Research Question: Which content pages show the strongest measurable signals of future decline, and how can those pages be ranked for human review using historical search and engagement data?
Researcher Pakiza Hassan
Degree BS Artificial Intelligence

Research Summary

Abstract

This research investigates whether historical search and engagement signals can help identify content pages that deserve earlier human review because they later experience a substantial decline in search visibility.

Using March 2026 FlyRank internship warehouse data, the workflow separates historical features from future outcomes, applies client-grouped validation, audits potential leakage, trains an interpretable Decision Tree classifier, and converts predicted decline-risk probabilities into a ranked review queue.

The final model achieved 72.71% accuracy, 57.98% precision, 12.71% recall, and a 20.85% F1 score on held-out clients. Results are interpreted as directional decision-support rather than proof that a particular page requires refreshing.

01 — Research Problem

Prioritizing Content Review at Scale

Large content portfolios can contain thousands of pages, making it difficult for content teams to inspect every page equally. Machine learning can support this workflow by highlighting pages with stronger measured risk signals while leaving the final editorial decision with human reviewers.

Decision to Support

The system answers a prioritization question: which pages should a content team investigate first? It does not automatically decide that a page should be rewritten, deleted, merged, or refreshed.

03 — Methodology

Time-Aware ML Workflow

Historical information and future outcomes are separated to reduce leakage risk. The model receives only information available during the historical observation period, while later data is reserved for defining the future outcome.

1

Historical Window

March 1–21, 2026 is used to create historical model features.

2

Future Outcome

March 22–31 is used only to measure subsequent impression change.

3

Grouped Validation

Clients are separated between training and testing to reduce memorization.

4

Ranked Actions

Risk probabilities are translated into practical human-review priorities.


Historical Features

DAILY_IMPRESSIONS CTR AVG_POSITION DAILY_SESSIONS ENGAGEMENT_RATE DAILY_SCROLL_EVENTS

Target Definition

A page is labeled as an observed future-decline case when average daily future impressions fall by at least 20% compared with its historical daily impressions.

Future-window variables are not used as predictive features.

Useful precision, limited recall

05 — Action Engine

Ranked Review Queue

Decline-risk probabilities are converted into priority categories to help content teams decide where to investigate first. Recommendations are accompanied by reason codes so reviewers can understand the measured signals behind each priority.

How to Read the Queue

Review First identifies the strongest measured decline-risk signals. Monitor represents moderate signals that may warrant observation. Lower Priority represents weaker measured risk under the current model.

These categories prioritize investigation only. They do not instruct the system to automatically rewrite, delete, merge, or publish content.

06 — Honest Framing

Limitations

The system is intentionally framed as decision-support. The available evidence supports prioritization, but it does not establish that refreshing a page will cause improved search performance.

Proxy Outcome

Future impression decline is measurable, but it is not a ground-truth label stating that a page requires refreshing.

Short Observation Period

The analysis uses March 2026 only, limiting conclusions about longer-term behavior.

Observational Evidence

Associations between historical signals and future decline do not establish causation.

Low Recall

Recall is 12.71%, so many observed future-decline pages remain undetected.

GA4 Coverage

Historical GA4 availability is approximately 10.16%, so engagement signals require cautious interpretation.

Generalization

Model behavior may change across new clients, content types, search conditions, and later reporting periods.

Practical Takeaway

The main value of this system is prioritization rather than automation.

Historical visibility, CTR, search position, and supporting engagement signals can help identify a smaller set of pages that deserve earlier investigation.

Machine learning ranks the opportunities; human reviewers investigate the context and decide whether monitoring, updating, rewriting, or no action is appropriate.
