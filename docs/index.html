Research Summary

Abstract

This study develops an interpretable machine-learning workflow for prioritizing content pages that may deserve earlier human review.

Using March 2026 FlyRank internship warehouse data, the workflow separates historical features from future outcomes, applies client-grouped validation, audits possible data leakage, trains a shallow Decision Tree model, and converts decline-risk probabilities into a ranked review queue.

The outcome proxy identifies pages whose average daily future impressions decline by at least 20% relative to their historical period.

The final held-out evaluation achieved 72.71% accuracy, 57.98% precision, 12.71% recall, and an F1 score of 20.85%. The findings are interpreted as directional decision-support rather than proof that a page requires refreshing.

01 — Research Problem

Prioritizing Content Review at Scale

Large content portfolios can contain thousands of pages, while content teams have limited time for manual investigation. This research asks whether historical search and engagement measurements can help narrow that workload to pages showing stronger measured decline-risk signals.

Decision to Support

The system is designed to answer one practical question: which pages should a content team investigate first?

It is not intended to automatically rewrite, delete, merge, or publish content.

03 — Methodology

Time-Aware ML Workflow

The analysis separates historical information from future outcomes to reduce leakage. Model features are created only from the historical period, while later data is used only to construct the future outcome and evaluate performance.

1

Historical Window

March 1–21, 2026 is used to construct historical predictive features.

2

Future Outcome

March 22–31 is reserved for measuring subsequent impression movement.

3

Grouped Validation

Training and testing clients remain separate, producing zero client overlap.

4

Action Engine

Model probabilities are converted into interpretable review priorities and reason codes.



Historical Features

DAILY_IMPRESSIONS CTR AVG_POSITION DAILY_SESSIONS ENGAGEMENT_RATE DAILY_SCROLL_EVENTS

A page is treated as an observed future-decline case when average daily impressions fall by at least 20% in the future period. Future-period variables are not provided to the model as predictive features.

Reason Codes

Reason codes help a human reviewer understand which historical signals contributed to a page's review priority.

HIGH_VISIBILITY_AT_RISK LOW_CLICK_CAPTURE WEAK_SEARCH_POSITION LOW_ENGAGEMENT DECLINE_RISK_SIGNAL

These labels explain the measured signal behind prioritization. They do not prove why performance changed or prescribe a specific editorial action.

Human review remains required: seasonality, changing demand, technical issues, indexing behavior, analytics coverage, and page purpose should be investigated before changing content.
06 — Honest Framing

Limitations

The available evidence supports prioritization, not causal claims. These limitations should be considered when interpreting both the model and the ranked recommendations.

Proxy Target
Future impression decline is measurable, but it is not a ground-truth label stating that content requires refreshing.
Short Time Window
The study covers March 2026 only.
Observational Study
Association does not prove that refreshing a page causes improved search performance.
Low Recall
Recall is 12.71%, meaning many observed future-decline cases remain undetected.
Analytics Coverage
Average historical GA4 coverage is approximately 10.16%, so engagement-based interpretation requires caution.
Generalization
Performance may change for new clients, content categories, search conditions, or future reporting periods.

Practical Takeaway

The main value of this system is prioritization rather than automatic content editing.

Historical visibility, CTR, search position, and supporting engagement measurements can help identify a smaller group of pages that deserve earlier investigation.

Machine learning ranks the opportunities; human reviewers investigate the context and decide whether monitoring, refreshing, rewriting, or no action is appropriate.
